Addendum · 24 August 2026

What changed since the report

Three things came back after I finished the main document. The two about Plessy are good news. The one about ISL sets a deadline.

The short version

Plessy's technology practice and its phonics program both checked out. Every curricular explanation I had for its weak numbers is now closed. That makes the numbers stranger, and it means whatever is wrong isn't what the school teaches.

Separately, ISL turns out to have a hard entry deadline. After first grade it requires students to already read, write and speak French or Spanish. The 2027–28 lottery is River's last chance to enter that school, ever.

What I'd do differently: don't rush a transfer before 4 September. Rank carefully in November instead.

One

Plessy’s screen time is fine

Appendix C sets three questions for any school: minutes per day, which programs, and whether screen time adds to teacher-led instruction or replaces it. His teacher answered all three unprompted.

During our Learning Lab block, students are placed into small groups and rotate through four centers: seatwork, ABC Match, Play-Doh, and computer. Students spend about 15 minutes at each center.

During this time, I'm also able to pull small groups to work on individual literacy skills and provide additional support as needed. For the computer center, we will begin the year using a free program called Starfall, which focuses on letter recognition, letter sounds, and other early literacy skills.

The laptops will be used as a supplemental tool to reinforce the skills we are learning in class while allowing students to practice independently.

Mrs. Dartez, kindergarten teacher, Homer Plessy Community School

Fifteen minutes. One of four stations, next to seatwork, letter manipulatives and Play-Doh. On letter sounds. And the detail she probably didn't think was the headline: the rotation is what frees her to run small-group literacy instruction. The computer creates teacher time rather than eating it.

That clears the NAEYC displacement standard, since play and fine-motor work are still in the rotation. It also clears the UNESCO finding, where the failure mode is devices arriving and teachers stepping back. Criterion four passes.

Two limits worth holding. This describes kindergarten, and fifteen minutes at a center looks nothing like a one-to-one laptop in fourth or sixth grade, so the question needs asking again each year. And I have no equivalent answer from ISL, which still publishes no device policy at all.

Report change

Section II: Plessy's screens rating moves from WEAK to STRONG, and it no longer fails any criterion.

Two

Plessy runs Fundations

This is the bigger finding. Section II flagged one real gap: Wit & Wisdom's own publisher says the program doesn't systematically teach young children to decode and recommends pairing it with a separate foundational-skills program. Plessy hadn't published what fills that gap.

For letter recognition and letter sounds, I use a program called Fundations during whole-group instruction. It provides consistent practice with letter identification, sounds, and early phonics skills.

Mrs. Dartez

Fundations is Wilson Language Training's structured literacy program. Tier 1, grounded in the science of reading, and rated all-green by EdReports for high-quality foundational skills instruction. Thirty to thirty-five minutes daily, Pre-K through third grade, and built to run alongside a core literature-based language arts program.

Fundations is the program Wit & Wisdom is meant to pair with, and that pairing isn't accidental. Florida approved the combined package of Fundations plus Wit & Wisdom as an approved ELA textbook, and Oregon approved the same combination. Plessy is running the pairing states adopt as a unit.

Every curricular explanation is now closed

I went into this expecting to find something missing. Here is what I found instead:

  • English curriculum. Wit & Wisdom. Top EdReports ratings, Louisiana Tier 1, K–8.
  • Phonics. Fundations. All-green, thirty-plus minutes daily.
  • Mathematics. Illustrative Mathematics. Problem-based, discourse-driven.
  • Science. Amplify Science.
  • Arts. Dance, music, theater and visual art daily, with strings and band at Tremé.
  • Screens. Fifteen supplemental minutes, structured to enable small-group teaching.

Plessy now passes all five criteria on confirmed evidence. It has the strongest documented curriculum of any school in the report, public or private, including Audubon and ISL, neither of which publishes anything comparable.

And the cohort numbers are still 67 in reading and 49 in mathematics, against a citywide white benchmark of 82 and 72.

What this means

The curriculum does not explain Plessy's results. That moves the explanation into implementation, staffing, leadership or history, none of which I can see from outside. It also means the fix, if there is one, is a management problem, and those can turn around fast.

Report change

Section II: the paragraph beginning “Two things cut against it” no longer applies. Plessy is a clean five-of-five fit. The first of the two questions for Plessy in Section V is answered.

Three

ISL has a door that closes

From the school's admissions brochure, eligibility requirements:

For Kindergarten and 1st Grade: No experience with French or Spanish is required. A language assessment is required for all first-grade applicants who apply after September 1st, as part of the admissions process.

For 2nd – 8th Grade: Students must already read, write, speak, and understand French or Spanish. A language assessment is required prior to admission.

The 2027–28 Main Round would place River in first grade, which needs no prior language. Every round after that would place him in second grade or higher, where fluency is mandatory and he'd have no way to have acquired it.

November is the last time we can rank ISL. There is no coming back to it in third grade if Plessy's numbers stay flat.

That argues both ways, and I want to be honest about both. It removes the urgency to move him in the next two weeks, since a first-grade entry next fall is fully open. It also makes the November ranking one-shot, which is a different kind of pressure.

Other useful things in the brochure

Their own numbers back up the immersion argument. ISL says over 75% of students enter at kindergarten from English-speaking homes and leave 8th grade bilingual or trilingual. Three quarters of the children taking that third-grade English test started with no French or Spanish. The grade-3 dip in the comparison chart is what the design predicts.

Scale and composition. About 1,100 students across three campuses. 38% African American, 33% Latinx/Hispanic, 26% White, 2% Asian/Pacific, 1% American Indian. Over 200 staff from 33 countries, fluent in 26 languages. Twenty-six years operating, chartered before Katrina.

Campuses confirmed. St. Roch is K–3 at 2518 Arts Street. Uptown is 4th–8th at 1400 Camp Street, across from Coliseum Square. Westbank is K–5 and Spanish only. Mandarin gets added in middle school, so the trilingual claim is real.

Transportation confirmed. Yellow bus for grades K–5, RTA passes for 6–8. Requires living in Orleans Parish, more than a mile from campus, and agreeing to the transportation handbook. We qualify on all three.

One thing to ask about. ISL lists a COSEBOC award for "the effective elimination of the achievement gap for boys of color." The spread table in Section III puts its white-to-Black gap at 32 points. The award is undated and may be old. If that framing matters to either of us, it's a fair question for a tour.

Report change

Section II and the November ranking in Section V: the first-grade entry rule becomes a hard deadline rather than a footnote. Confirm it in writing, since this brochure is one cycle old.

Four

What I’d do now

The main report told you to call ISL this week about a kindergarten seat and move him before 4 September. I don't think that anymore, and I want to say why rather than just quietly change the ranking.

That recommendation rested on three unknowns: Plessy's device practice, Plessy's phonics, and whether missing ISL now closed the door. All three came back, and all three point the same direction. His device practice is good. His phonics program is the best available. And the ISL door stays open through first grade.

There's a fourth thing the data can't show. The teacher answering these emails is prompt, specific, and knows her materials cold. She named Fundations without hedging and offered to help us reinforce it at home. That is what good implementation looks like at the level River experiences, and no cohort table shows it.

Revised recommendation

Don't transfer before 4 September. Pulling a five-year-old three weeks into kindergarten, giving up daily arts and a teacher who's already engaging with us, to reach a school that ties Audubon on outcomes and loses to Plessy on curriculum, is a worse trade than it looked a week ago.

Do rank deliberately in November. Audubon, Willow, ISL, Plessy. Same order as the report. The stakes on ISL are higher than I knew, since it's the only shot.

Watch two numbers. Next spring's K–3 literacy screener for Plessy, and its 2027 cohort figures against the parish benchmarks of 82 and 72.

Five

Still open

When did Plessy adopt Fundations? This is the question I'd most like answered. The LEAP data covering grades 3 through 8 describes children who started kindergarten roughly between 2018 and 2023. If Fundations arrived recently, those numbers measure a school that no longer exists in this form. A two-year answer would change how much weight the LEAP figures deserve. A ten-year answer would leave them standing.

It's an easy ask, and casual: how long have you all been using Fundations?

ISL's device policy. Still nothing published. Now that Plessy has set a high bar with a specific answer, the same three questions are worth putting to ISL on a tour.

Plessy in the upper grades. Everything confirmed here is kindergarten. What Learning Lab looks like in fourth grade, and what happens at the Tremé campus for grades 5 through 8, is unknown.

The ISL language rule. Confirm the first-grade exemption in writing before the November round. The brochure is from the 2025 cycle.
